# Context Map
## Sales Management System — SynkroTech SAS

**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** August 2026

---

## Purpose

Before deciding on the architecture style (see `adr/adr-001-architecture.md`), this document identifies the **bounded contexts** of the SynkroTech SAS business using business language — not based on database tables — and evaluates, for each one, whether it should become an independent service or remain grouped with another context.

---

## 1. Identified bounded contexts

### 1.1 Authentication and Users

**What it is:** Identity management for internal system users (SynkroTech SAS employees: administrators, salespersons, inventory staff), their credentials, and their roles/permissions.

**Why it is a distinct context:** The concept of a "user who logs in" has no business relationship with a "client who purchases". They are two distinct entities with different lifecycles: a user is created when an employee is hired; a client is created when someone makes their first purchase.

**Independent service?** **Yes.**
**Justification:** It is a cross-cutting context — all other contexts depend on it to authorize operations, but it does not depend on any of them. Isolating it prevents security logic from being duplicated or coupled to the Clients domain.

---

### 1.2 Clients

**What it is:** Information about SynkroTech SAS clients: contact details, and their existence as an entity to associate them with sales.

**Why it is a distinct context:** Its lifecycle and business rules (validating identity documents, preventing duplicates, deactivating instead of deleting) are its own, and do not depend on how products or sales are managed.

**Independent service?** **Yes.**
**Justification:** Low coupling with Products (they do not share business rules). Its only touchpoint with Sales is a simple query ("does this client exist?"), which is easily resolved via an API without needing to share a data model.

---

### 1.3 Products and Inventory

**What it is:** Product catalog, categories, and available stock control.

**Why it is a distinct context:** It has its own business rules and a higher probability of frequent changes (new categories, stock adjustments, new products) than the other contexts, and it is queried with high frequency (every sale needs to check stock).

**Independent service?** **Yes.**
**Justification:** It is the context with the greatest need for frequent querying and concurrency control (preventing the sale of an out-of-stock product). Isolating it allows it to be optimized or scaled independently if the catalog continues to grow, without affecting Clients or Sales.

---

### 1.4 Sales (includes Reports)

**What it is:** Record of commercial transactions (sales and their details), orchestration with Clients and Products at the time of sale, and generation of reports (daily, monthly, top-selling products) based on that same transactional information.

**Are Reports their own context, or part of Sales?**
Treating Reports as a separate context was evaluated, but **it was decided that Reports are part of the same business context as Sales**, not a separate context:
- Reports are calculated **exclusively** from the data already generated by Sales (they do not consume information from Clients or Products directly, only from the transactions that Sales already possesses).
- There is no business rule for Reports that is independent of Sales rules — it is, in essence, an aggregated view of the same transactional information.
- There is no need to scale Reports differently from Sales: both grow at the same pace (a new sale is, at the same time, new data for the reports).

**Independent service?** **Yes** (Sales, including Reports, as a single service).
**Justification:** Sales is the context that depends the most on the other two (Clients and Products), so isolating it allows it to be the sole orchestration point between them, without Clients or Products needing to know that sales exist.

---

## 2. Context map summary

| Context | Independent service? | Depends on | Depended on by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authentication and Users | Yes | — | Clients, Products, Sales (JWT validation) |
| Clients | Yes | Auth (JWT validation) | Sales (existence check) |
| Products and Inventory | Yes | Auth (JWT validation) | Sales (stock and price check) |
| Sales (+ Reports) | Yes | Auth, Clients, Products | — |

**Total: 4 contexts → 4 microservices.** No context was grouped with another except Reports, which was integrated as an internal module of Sales because it is part of the same business context (not a simplification due to repository limits, but a true domain decision).

---

## 3. Criteria used to decide "service vs. grouped"

For each context, the following was evaluated:

1. **Independent change:** Does this context change with a different frequency and for different reasons than the others?
2. **Business coupling:** Does it share business rules or only exchange specific data via queries?
3. **Need to scale differently:** Might this context need more resources or availability than the others?
4. **Data ownership:** Who is the natural owner of this information — can a single responsible context be identified?

The 4 identified contexts meet these criteria independently of each other, which supports the decision to separate them as services (formalized in `adr/adr-001-architecture.md`). Reports, on the other hand, do not meet any of the 4 criteria differently from Sales, which is why it remains grouped with it.
